Why the Standard Advice Falls Short

Three reasons you can do everything "right" and still be up at 2AM

None of this means you're doing something wrong. It usually means the advice was never diagnostic in the first place.

01 · The symptom trap

One remedy, aimed at the wrong system

Gripe water has never passed a randomized controlled trial against placebo. Simethicone gas drops failed to show a statistically significant benefit in a Cochrane review. Both treat gas as the problem. For a baby whose primary system is nervous system dysregulation or acoustic overload, gas was never the actual driver, so neither product had a mechanism to work through.

02 · The reassurance trap

True, and not the same thing as useful

Most colic does resolve with time, and reassurance that it will pass is honest. But "it will pass" doesn't tell you which of the three systems is active tonight, it just tells you to keep enduring one of them a little longer than you might need to.

03 · The one-size trap

A protocol built for someone else's baby

The environment fix that settles an acoustic-overload baby does nothing for a baby whose issue is gut inflammation. Almost no piece of generic colic content tells you which baby it's actually written for. Yours might not be it.

The Evidence, Not Just the Credential

Named studies you can go verify yourself.

We'd rather you check these than take our word for it.

Savino et al., Pediatrics, 2010

Randomized controlled trial reporting a 74% reduction in daily crying time by Day 21 using a specific L. reuteri strain. Conducted in breastfed infants specifically: if your baby is formula-fed or mixed-fed, this particular trial's population doesn't match yours, and the Blueprint's formula-feeding track uses different, broader evidence instead.

Network meta-analysis, 32 RCTs, 2,242 infants

Comparing colic interventions head to head, this specific L. reuteri strain ranked first across the pooled trials. We name the strain publicly because the research is public. The exact dose and timing protocol calibrated by feeding method is inside the Blueprint.

Ellwood et al., BMJ Open, 2020 · Hjern et al., Acta Paediatrica, 2020

Two independent systematic reviews reaching the same conclusion as Savino 2010, which is the part that matters more than any single trial: it's been replicated, not just published once.
