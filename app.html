Brick 1 • Foundations
What Colic Actually Is (and Isn't)

Colic is not an internal disease, a stomach malformation, or a diagnosis of failure. Since 1954, pediatricians have defined it by a crying timeline: Wessel's Rule of Three.

Wessel et al., 1954 (Pediatrics): Crying for >3 hours a day, >3 days a week, for >3 weeks in an otherwise healthy, growing baby. Colic usually peaks around week 6 and eases naturally by 3 to 4 months.

Brick 2 • Differential Triage
Colic or Reflux? (How to Tell)

Reflux is the condition most often confused with colic. In reflux, the stomach valve is immature, causing stomach contents to flow back up. Use this clinical check to tell them apart:

Sign Colic Reflux
Timing Often worse in the evening (cluster crying) Often worse right after feeds, any time of day
Spit-Up Not a main feature Frequent spit-up or painful wet burps
Back Arching Can happen during acute crying spells Often happens during or right after a feed
Feeding Usually feeds normally and eagerly May arch away or refuse bottle/breast in pain
The 5-Sign Cow's Milk Allergy Check: Blood or mucus in stool, severe eczema, projectile vomiting, hives, or poor weight gain. If present, contact your pediatrician for an allergy review, not simple colic soothing.

Brick 3 • Mechanics Physics
Bottle Pacing, Venting & Latch Physics

When feeding goes too fast, excess swallowed air causes abdominal discomfort later in the evening.

2025 Paced Feeding Trial (29 Pairs): Pacing reliably slows milk flow and extends feed duration to match breastfeeding pace without reducing total nutrition.
JAMA Network Open, 2026 (1,055 Infants): Bottom-vented bottles reduced immediate post-feed fussing, but overall colic rates remained similar across bottle designs. Mechanics matter more than expensive bottle brands.

Brick 4 • Gut & Digestion
The Honest Probiotic Picture (L. reuteri)

The research on Lactobacillus reuteri DSM 17938 is genuinely mixed, and depends on how your baby is fed:

Savino et al., 2010 (Pediatrics): Found less crying by day 21 with L. reuteri in 50 exclusively breastfed infants.
Sung et al., 2014 (BMJ): A larger trial in 167 mixed and formula-fed infants found no benefit over placebo. Formula parents should lead with feeding mechanics and soothing steps, not probiotics.
Biagioli et al., 2016 (Cochrane Review): Simethicone gas drops (Mylicon) showed no statistically significant difference from placebo.

Brick 5 • Sensory Science
Sound Safety & The Tiger Hold

A newborn's sensory nervous system is brand new. Dim rooms, continuous brown noise, and firm holds give comfort in the moment:

Hugh et al., 2014 & AAP 2023 Technical Report: Tested 14 sound machines; every one exceeded the safe 50 dBA nursery limit at max volume. Sound should always be quiet, low, on a timer, and across the room.
Barr et al., 1991 (Pediatrics): Extra carrying does not shorten the underlying course of colic. The Tiger Hold is an essential comfort hold for tonight, not a cure.

Brick 6 • Outlook & Parent Health
Long-Term Outcomes & Parent Wellbeing

What the latest research says about your baby's future and your own mental health:

Liao et al., 2026 (Scientific Reports, 19,191 Infants): Found a statistical association with childhood gut sensitivity, but no evidence of lasting cognitive, behavioral, or developmental harm. The large majority of colicky babies grow up completely healthy.
Pediatrics Systematic Review, 2026 (2,508 Parents): Structured parent education with actionable coping steps measurably reduced parental depression and anxiety, protecting the caregiver while the infant matures.

Safety-Critical Screening

When to Call the Doctor Right Away

Colic is normal crying in a healthy, growing infant. Stop and seek medical care if you observe any of these warning signs:

• Fever under 3 months: 100.4°F (38°C) or higher.
• Repeated forceful vomiting: Projectile vomiting, or green/yellow bile.
• Blood in stool or vomit: Any visible blood or dark flecks.
• Dehydration signs: Fewer than 6 wet diapers in 24 hours, dry lips, sunken fontanelle.
• Lethargy or weak cry: Very hard to wake, floppy, or unusual high-pitched cry.
Disclaimer: This app is educational and does not provide clinical diagnoses. Always consult your pediatrician before altering infant care.
