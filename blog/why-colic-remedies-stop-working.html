Why Colic Remedies Stop Working (And What's Actually Going On)

July 31, 20267 min readBy Vincent, Epidemiologist

By week five, most parents have a familiar list: gripe water, gas drops, two or three hold techniques, a sound machine, maybe a formula switch. Each one seemed to work, briefly, and then stopped. If that's where you are, the natural conclusion is "nothing works for my baby" or "I must be doing it wrong." Neither is accurate. The remedies did exactly what they were designed to do. It just wasn't enough, and the reason why is mechanical, not a reflection of your effort.

Why techniques work temporarily, and only partially

Colic crying is rarely produced by a single mechanism. Most presentations trace back to some combination of three overlapping systems: gut microbiome imbalance, nervous system dysregulation, and acoustic or sensory overload. Any single technique, gas drops, a specific hold, a sound machine, addresses at most one of those three. If it happens to be the primary driver, you'll see real relief. If it's a secondary factor, you'll see partial, temporary relief before the untreated systems reassert themselves. That's not the technique failing. That's the technique doing its narrow job correctly while the rest of the problem continues unaddressed.

The compounding problem nobody mentions

There's a second layer that makes this worse over time. Each remedy that doesn't hold raises parental stress and anxiety a little further, understandably. What often gets missed is that a parent's own physiological stress state transmits to an infant through touch, tone, and handling, not metaphorically, but through observable, measurable pathways. A baby held by an increasingly anxious parent is receiving an additional stress signal on top of whatever their own system was already dealing with. The remedies aren't just failing to resolve the original problem, the failed attempts themselves can be adding to it.

What to do instead of trying the next remedy

The instinct after remedy four or five is usually to try remedy six. That instinct is understandable and usually the wrong move. The higher-leverage step is identifying which of the three systems is actually primary before adding anything else to the list, because the right technique for the wrong system was never going to resolve much, no matter how many times or how carefully it's tried.

This is also where it's worth being direct about the emotional side, not just the mechanical one. None of this happened because you missed something obvious or didn't try hard enough. A multi-system problem approached one system at a time is going to look like a string of failures even when every individual attempt was reasonable. The fix isn't trying harder at the same approach. It's identifying which system is actually driving tonight's crying before spending another week on the wrong one.
