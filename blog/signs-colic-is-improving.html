Signs Your Baby's Colic Is Improving

Improvement rarely arrives as one dramatic quiet night. It shows up first in small, easy-to-miss shifts. Here's what to actually watch for.

4 minute read · Journey & Tracking

Improvement shows up first in duration, then frequency, then predictability. Rarely all at once.

Improvement almost never looks like the movie version, one night everything is suddenly fine. It shows up first in small changes that are easy to dismiss as a fluke, especially when you're too exhausted to notice a trend forming.

What actually tends to show up first, in roughly this order:

  • Crying episodes get shorter before they get less frequent. A 90-minute evening episode becoming a 60-minute one is real progress, even if it's still happening every day. This is often the very first shift, and it's easy to miss if you're only counting whether crying happened at all rather than how long it lasted
  • A technique starts working faster. If Tiger Hold used to take ten minutes to take effect and now takes four, that's a nervous system becoming more responsive to calming input, a meaningful sign even before total crying time drops much
  • Sleep stretches lengthen, even slightly. Going from 45-minute stretches to 90-minute stretches is a real, trackable improvement, well before anyone would call the sleep "good"
  • The pattern becomes more predictable. Colic that's unpredictable, sometimes 5pm, sometimes 8pm, sometimes twice a day, tends to consolidate into a single, more predictable window as things improve

This is exactly why tracking matters more than it might seem worth the effort. A single night that felt slightly better is easy to write off as coincidence or exhaustion-distorted memory. Four consecutive nights with measurably shorter episodes, logged with actual times, is a real trend, and it's usually only visible in a log, not in memory alone.

If it's been several weeks with no version of these signs at all, not smaller improvements, just no movement, that's worth revisiting whether you're addressing the actual primary system for your baby. See the root-cause guide or retake the assessment; colic that's genuinely not responding to a gut-focused approach, for instance, may be more centrally a nervous-system or feeding-mechanics pattern instead. And if the baby is improving but you're not, that's worth its own attention too; see Colic and Postpartum Mental Health.
